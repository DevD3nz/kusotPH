The story

KusotPh didn't start in an office.
It started at a laundry counter, on the customer's side.

  1. 01

    “Balikan n'yo na lang po bukas.”

    I dropped off a full bag of clothes at my neighborhood laundry shop and paid. I asked when I could get it. “Bukas po siguro, hapon.” Tomorrow, maybe, sometime in the afternoon. No exact time and no number to text. All I had was a small paper stub.

  2. 02

    Two trips for one bag.

    The next afternoon I walked back. “Dinadryer pa po.” Still drying. Nobody had told me, because nobody could. I went home empty-handed, came back the day after, and went to work in yesterday's shirt. It wasn't the shop's fault. They had no way to reach me.

  3. 03

    Then I thought about it from the shop's side of the counter.

    Every customer like me costs a shop something.

    • A phone call the staff answers while a load waits to be folded.
    • A bag on the shelf that nobody comes back for, taking up space for weeks.
    • A customer who quietly tries the shop down the street next time, and never says why.

    None of it shows up in sales. The shop just ends up with fewer suki than it should.

  4. 04

    So I built the shop I wished I'd walked into.

    Kusot is how we scrub clothes by hand: simple, hands-on, done by the person at the tub. KusotPh works the same way. The staff takes the order on a phone, and from that moment the customer knows what's happening:

    • A ready-by time printed on every claim ticket.
    • A live receipt link that shows Received → Washing → Drying → Ready.
    • A text the moment it's Ready, and for pickup orders, reminders if the bag is still waiting after 3, 7 and 30 days.
  5. 05

    Then I built the rest of the shop around it.

    Clear updates for customers need clean records behind the counter. So KusotPh also keeps the cash drawer honest, tracks who worked which shift, and lets an owner see every branch from a phone instead of driving between them to ask.

How it works

One board. Every load. Always in the right stage.

Staff move each order along with a tap. The customer's link updates as they go.

  1. 0

    To collect

    Home pickup: a rider brings the dirty laundry in.

  2. 1

    Received

    Weighed, priced, ticket printed with a ready-by time.

  3. 2

    Washing

    In the machine. Flagged Late if the time passes.

  4. 3

    Drying

    Almost there. The customer can see it too.

  5. 4

    Ready

    Customer gets a text. Pickup orders get reminders after 3, 7 and 30 days.

  6. 5

    Out for delivery

    A rider takes it. If nobody's home, it goes back to Ready and the attempt is counted.

  7. 6

    Claimed / Delivered

    Staff release only when the balance is zero. The owner can override.

  • Staff can undo a step within 15 minutes; the owner any time
  • Failed delivery goes back to Ready, attempt counted
  • Cancel only by the owner, with a reason, before any payment is taken
  • Decimal kilos or per piece, plus rush & delivery fees

Under the hood

A phone app that's really a website.

KusotPh is a progressive web app: a Laravel API with a React front end, installed to the home screen like an app. It's set up for one shop, with one or several branches under one owner account.

  • Works on any phone or tablet; a thermal printer is optional
  • One shop per install, so its data stays its own
  • Staff sign in with a PIN at the counter

Questions

Things people ask.

Can my shop use it?

Yes. Message me on Facebook and tell me about your shop. I'll show you how it works on a phone and we can talk from there.

Does it need a computer or a POS machine?

No. KusotPh runs in the browser on any phone or tablet and can be installed to the home screen like an app. A thermal printer is optional, for claim tickets.

How do customers get the text when their laundry is ready?

Through an SMS gateway that's switched on per shop. Once it's on, KusotPh texts customers when their order is received, washing and ready, and reminds pickup customers if the bag is still unclaimed after 3, 7 and 30 days.

Does it take GCash payments?

It records payments made by cash, GCash, bank transfer or other methods against each order, including deposits and cash on delivery. It is not a payment gateway; customers pay the shop directly the way they do now.

What about shops with more than one branch?

Each branch has its own orders, cash drawer and staff, and the owner sees all of them together in the Command Center.

What if the staff aren't techy?

The order board is built for the counter: big buttons, PIN login, one tap to move a load to the next stage.
